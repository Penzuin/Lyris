# T9: Open questions, options for the user

Two questions from the Open Questions tab of `Game_Concept.xlsx`. Each has three options to choose from, and a recommendation at the end. Nothing here is decided until the user says so.

Anything marked **[new]** is not in Lyris canon yet and needs the user's OK. Everything else comes from the companion data (`lore.json`, `bestiary.json`, `systems.json`).

## What canon already fixes

These hold in every option:

- **Three regions.** The Quiet Reach in the north, running from the strait up to the northern ice. Saltreach (and its green eastern side, Greengrace) on the west continent. Sunreach in the south-east. A strait divides the Reach from the other two.
- **The mist has only ever touched the Quiet Reach.** Canon says Saltreach and Sunreach have never seen it. It says nothing about the open sea.
- **One cluster at the heart.** Moonharbor, the Observatory on the hill above it, and Ilwen's Lighthouse on the Mistwater headland sit together on the Reach coast. The star fell into the sea off Moonharbor, and drowned Aurenhal lies under the bay.
- **The Delvers' Gate is on land,** in the north at the end of the North Road, and the Hollowdeep runs down from it.
- **The Old Shore** is somewhere over the sea. Its ships stopped coming, and canon never says in which direction it lies.
- **Useful hooks:** the Teeth (a reef off the bay), a walled town far north that "still answers with bells", volcano reefs, polar seas, and the starless Hollow in the far south that Corin Ashby sailed to chart.

Canon also gives us this: **the deepest mystery lies right under the safest harbour.** Intensity grows the farther out you sail, and also the deeper you dive beneath Moonharbor's bay. The options below shape the first; the second is the same in all of them.

*Distances assume a small sloop at about 1 km every 100 seconds.*

# Question 1: How do the seas and islands fit around the three regions?

## Option A: The Inner Sea

**The picture.** The strait is wide: an inner sea scattered with islands, with the Quiet Reach on its north shore and Saltreach and Sunreach on the south. Moonharbor sits on the Reach's southern coast **[new placement]**, and on clear days the other regions show as blue lines on the horizon. The strait opens at both ends onto open ocean, where the charts stop. The concept sheet already hints at this: "the sea and strait between them become the sailing world."

```
        snow . glacier . the bells?  (far deep, north via the west mouth)
   ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
   ~~      T H E   Q U I E T   R E A C H   (mainland)    ~~
   ~~   Delvers' Gate ^ North Road                       ~~
   ~~      Observatory  *  MOONHARBOR  ) Lighthouse      ~~
 W ~~        the Teeth  .  bay (Aurenhal below)          ~~  E
 mouth  o   o  home waters  o    o                    mouth
 ~~~~  o  o    o   M I D D L E   S E A S  o   o  o   ~~~~~~
   ~~   o    o    (the strait's island chains)   o     ~~
   ~~  SALTREACH | GREENGRACE        SUNREACH            ~~
   ~~  (west continent)              (south-east)        ~~
   ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
          far deep: open ocean, south toward the Hollow
```

**Lit and unlit waters.** The mist comes off the Reach. At night it rolls south over the strait and thins toward the far shores, so the north half of the inner sea is where lamps matter most. The south coasts stay mist-free, as canon says, but they are unlit, wild and uncharted, so their danger is different: reefs, storms, volcano waters.

**Intensity zones.**
- *Home waters:* Moonharbor bay and the lamp-lit isles along the Reach coast.
- *Middle seas:* the island chains across the strait, out to the southern coasts.
- *Far deep:* out through either mouth: north along the ice toward the town that answers with bells, or south into open ocean toward the Hollow.

**Where things sit.** Moonharbor, the Observatory and the Lighthouse on the north shore, with the lighthouse beam sweeping the strait. Aurenhal under the bay, the Delvers' Gate inland behind the town. The Old Shore lies beyond the east mouth **[new]**, the way Ysara's white ships might once have come.

**Distances and size.** About 8 x 6 km of sea. Crossing the strait takes 8 to 10 minutes; reaching a mouth takes about 15. That fits a 20 to 60 player server well: busy enough that ships meet, roomy enough for home islands.

**Pros:** closest to canon and the concept sheet; all three regions in view, which suits the server's region players; southern coasts become later port towns; two clear frontiers for the far deep.

**Cons:** a bounded sea can feel smaller than an open ocean; needs a reason the south is unlit but mist-free; Moonharbor facing south must agree with the server build.

## Option B: The Reach Skerries

**The picture.** The game stays in the Quiet Reach, where every story so far happens. Off its long coast lies a broken chain of islands, the Skerries **[new name]**: sea stacks, wooded isles and old herring coves cut off since the roads closed. The mainland is always beside you, full of villages whose last lamp went out. Saltreach and Sunreach are rumours across the strait, saved for expansions.

```
   ~~~~~~~~~~ ICE SEA (far deep): bergs, bells in the fog ~~~~~~~~~
   ~~  glacier ridge  ..  north coast skerries  o  o  o         ~~
   ~~  [Q U I E T   R E A C H]    o  o  (middle seas)  o        ~~
   ~~  grey mts  Delvers' Gate ^   o   o   o                    ~~
   ~~  river country  Observatory *  MOONHARBOR ) Lighthouse    ~~
   ~~  great lake     herring coves  bay  the Teeth  o  o       ~~
   ~~                        o home waters o                    ~~
   ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ the strait ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
         (Saltreach and Sunreach: beyond the edge of the chart)
```

**Lit and unlit waters.** The simplest fit with canon: the mist belongs to the Reach, and so does the whole map. Lit waters are Moonharbor's bay and any island a player relights. Every unlit cove is mist at night.

**Intensity zones.**
- *Home waters:* Moonharbor bay and the inshore isles inside the lighthouse beam.
- *Middle seas:* the Skerries up and down the coast, including the old herring coves and waystone isles.
- *Far deep:* north round the glacier into the ice sea, where Bergmaw drift and the bells ring.

**Where things sit.** The canon cluster in the middle of the coast. Aurenhal under the bay. The Delvers' Gate reached by landing and walking the North Road. The Old Shore is out past the ice **[new]**, and the grey-sailed ship the lighthouse keeper saw going north along the reef becomes a clue to the way.

**Distances and size.** About 6 x 4 km, a long thin map. Moonharbor to the ice is about 12 minutes. The smallest and cheapest to build, and the easiest for the first prototype.

**Pros:** matches canon perfectly; smallest world to build; the mainland gives free land exploration; the other regions can arrive later as new seas.

**Cons:** two of three regions offstage at launch; a coastline runs one way, so it can feel linear; less "ocean of islands" than the pitch promises.

## Option C: The Open Sea

**The picture.** The three regions are great lands at the edge of a wide ocean, and the game is that ocean. Moonharbor is the last lit harbour, and the lighthouse beam is the edge of safety. Beyond it, islands spread in rings: reefs, volcano isles, black-glass crater islands, wrecks, and far out, the Old Shore.

```
                    far deep: fog banks, storms, the Old Shore?
              o         o           o            o
        o        o   middle seas: island rings    o      o
   SALTREACH        o        o          o              o
   GREENGRACE    o     ( home waters: beam radius )       o
     (west)          o  ) Lighthouse  MOONHARBOR      o
                          QUIET REACH coast  *Obs
                       o       SUNREACH (south-east)    o
              o            o            o          o
                    far deep: south toward the Hollow
```

**Lit and unlit waters.** Canon only says the mist has never touched Saltreach or Sunreach *land*. Here, mist rises over all open water at night **[new, needs the user's OK]**. Distance from a lit lamp is the whole difficulty curve: a lamp you light on an island pushes the safe waters outward for everyone.

**Intensity zones.** Rings around Ilwen's Lighthouse.
- *Home waters:* inside the beam.
- *Middle seas:* the first rings of islands, where trade routes and other players' lamps grow.
- *Far deep:* past the last lamp, with storms, fog banks and newly surfaced islands.

**Where things sit.** The canon cluster on the Reach coast at the centre. Aurenhal under the bay. The Delvers' Gate inland. Saltreach and Sunreach as far coasts you can reach after a long voyage. The Old Shore at the outer edge, the final voyage.

**Distances and size.** The biggest: 16 x 16 km or more, ideally with streamed or generated outer rings. The beam's edge is a few minutes out; the far deep is 30 minutes or more. Needs fast travel between lit harbours.

**Pros:** the truest "ocean of islands", with Sea of Thieves scale; players' lamps visibly push the frontier out, making the Lamp Charter the core loop; endless room for surfacing-island events.

**Cons:** most expensive to build and risks empty ocean; mist over open sea is new canon; the regions become backdrop rather than places.

# Question 2: The central mystery

All three are built on the sleeping star that sings under the sea. In each, players progress by understanding, not by combat or XP. The "truth" lines are for designers; the "players hear" lines show how it stays a mystery in the game.

Shared tools in every option: the **Alembic notebook leaves** (some partly in Lyrin, decoded with the Cipher Book), the **sealed academy wings** (the Hall of Alembics and its seven empty plinths, the orrery's scratched seventh star, the Millstone), the **charts** that fill in as you understand, and the **sky**.

*A caution:* several threads stay open in canon (Maelis, Vell's question, what Vane heard, Corin). None of these options settles them, but check the chosen one against the user's private notes.

## Option 1: What the Song Is Asking

**The truth.** The song is not noise and not power. It is a question, the same one Oruth has been asking since it fell, and the mist is that question spreading through the dark looking for an answer. Vell, the Aurenn runner, once asked the star something back, and it answered. **[new]**

**Players hear:** *"Vane said it sounded interested. Interested in what?"*

**How players discover it**
- *Waystone isles:* the tuned rings were instruments for measuring the song. Playing each ring at the right hour gives one phrase.
- *Notebook leaves:* each Choir member's leaves record their note (Light, Life, Stone, Heat, Void, Motion, Tide), the same seven properties in alchemy.
- *Crater islands:* the violet crystal rings back when you sing the right phrase to it.
- *Sealed wings:* the orrery's seventh star and the seven plinths show the song has a shape. Each Great Work claimed opens a room.
- *Aurenhal:* deep in the bay, the Aurenn writing on what they took from the star, and the records of the five exiles who said "ask it".

**Mist and lamps.** The mist goes still around a lit lamp because a flame answers in the star's own language, light, and the song stops searching there. Lamps become replies, not walls.

**Ending for a shared world.** Once a server pieces together the whole question, players hold a **Night of Answering** at the lamps. Afterwards the mist still rises, but it listens: new islands and chart lines appear in reply to what players light and sing. Each season brings a new verse.

**Pros:** turns the mist from enemy into conversation, which suits calm by default; uses the seven notes and alchemy already in canon; endless new verses.

**Cons:** makes Oruth clearly "someone", which canon leaves open on purpose; music puzzles are hard for some players; sits close to the forbidden Hollow Star faith's belief.

## Option 2: The Forgetting Sea

**The truth.** Ysolde dreams the world, and every star is something she remembers. Since the Unbottled Night, the song has seeped into her dream as mist, and wherever no light is kept, she is forgetting. The real stars going out over the bay and the islands lost in the dark are the same thing: the world slipping out of her memory. **[new]**

**Players hear:** *"The fishers say the real stars are slowly going out. The fishers are usually right."*

**How players discover it**
- *The sky:* the uncharted constellations and the dark patches over the bay. Charting a constellation (canon: named by whoever charts it first) restores a star.
- *Islands that weren't there:* some islands only appear on the chart after you have learned their story somewhere else.
- *Notebook leaves:* the Order's star records show which stars have gone dark since the Night, and in what order.
- *Sealed wings:* the Observatory's dome and the Temple of the Sleeping Moon's records open as the sky is restored.
- *Ruined villages:* the Wardens' ledgers of 194 black circles; relighting a place's lamp brings back its name.

**Mist and lamps.** A kept light is an act of remembering. The Lantern, the pole star, is Ysara's first lamp lifted into the sky, so canon already links lamps and stars. Wherever a lamp burns, Ysolde remembers, and the mist cannot take that place.

**Ending for a shared world.** No single win. The server's lit lamps and charted stars *are* the world's memory. Shared milestones, like restoring a whole dark constellation, bring back a lost island for everyone. There is always something left to remember, and late joiners can still help.

**Pros:** explains why islands surface; makes lighting lamps and charting stars the progression, matching the Lamp Charter, stargazing and collecting; gentle, no countdown; never has to explain the star fully.

**Cons:** puts weight on Ysolde ("do not wake the Moon"), which the user must be happy with; little natural climax, so milestones must carry it; "forgetting" could turn sad if handled heavily.

## Option 3: The Fourth Loud Night

**The truth.** Before Aurenhal drowned there were Three Loud Nights. The same signs are coming back: the sky over the bay going dark, the Delvers' Gate waking, lamps that won't dim. The mist rises a little higher on a count, the same tally the Aurenn engine-wardens kept and the Tidewives still sing, and it is counting toward a fourth night. **[new]**

**Players hear:** *"The Tidewives sing the old count every morning. This year one of them stopped halfway."*

**How players discover it**
- *Tide records:* Aurenn tide-marks and coins from wrecks, each stamped with a tier, map how the old count worked.
- *Pryde's shop:* the clocks that strike together, a little out of step, are keeping the count. Learning to read them is a key step.
- *Notebook leaves:* Merrin Saltash's leaves show how she read the count as "turned".
- *Hollowmere and the coves:* the herring road's singing waymarks fell silent in an order that matches.
- *Sealed wings:* the Millstone room shows what the Order took the count to mean, and what it really meant.

**Mist and lamps.** The Remnant swore never to build a lamp that could not be put out. A lamp somebody chooses to light every night is light given, not taken, and the song gives way to it. Lamps are the opposite of the Aurenn's never-dimming lights.

**Ending for a shared world.** A server-wide event when the count runs out. What players have learned and lit decides it: the sea draws back gently and new islands rise, or the night is hard. Then a new count begins, so the server gets chapters.

**Pros:** strongest Outer Wilds feel, a clock you learn to read; a real climax and a memorable shared night; echoes canon history closely.

**Cons:** a countdown fights calm by default; players who join later miss the event; closest to the server's live plot (the Gate waking, the darkening sky), so most likely to clash with the user's private plans.

# Recommendations

**Recommendation for Question 1: Option A, the Inner Sea.** It is the reading the concept sheet already points to, keeps all three regions in view for the server's region players, and still has two open frontiers for the far deep. Option B's Skerries could serve as the first prototype slice of it.

**Recommendation for Question 2: Option 2, the Forgetting Sea.** It makes lighting lamps and charting stars the progression, explains newly surfaced islands, keeps the star a mystery, and never runs out for a persistent shared world.
